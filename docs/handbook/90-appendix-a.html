A Annotated bibliography

How to read this list. Entries marked ● were read in full while compiling this book. The rest were consulted through search summaries — enough to source a specific claim, not enough to recommend the whole document unseen. Where a figure in the text comes from a source, it comes from one of these; nothing is cited that was not actually retrieved. Anything described as measured on this machine comes from context.md and is not in this list.

Voice agent architecture and latency  Part I, Chapter 3

● Deepgram — Voice Agent Architecture: STT, LLM and TTS Pipeline Design
The single best source for the latency budget. Carries Twilio's 350/375/100 ms allocation and the 1,115 ms mouth-to-ear target, the observation that most recognition delay is endpointing rather than acoustic compute, and a clear bundled-versus-composed comparison table. Read the “every response accumulates three budgets” framing; skip the product specifics. deepgram.com/learn/voice-agent-architecture-stt-llm-tts-pipeline-design
Deepgram — Speech-to-Speech vs Cascade
The architecture decision stated as a decision. Source for “cascade for five or more tools with strict schema enforcement”. deepgram.com/learn/speech-to-speech-vs-cascade-voice-agent-architecture
Gradium — Cascaded Voice Agents vs Speech-to-Speech: Architecture Tradeoffs in 2026
Where the 300–500 ms versus 600–1,200 ms time-to-first-audio comparison comes from, plus the note that hybrids are emerging as a middle ground. gradium.ai/content/cascaded-voice-agent-vs-speech-to-speech-2026
AssemblyAI — Voice Agent Architecture, and The voice AI stack for building agents
Two overlapping overviews. The first is the better one on cascading versus streaming and the 3×–5× perceived-latency claim; the second is a survey of the provider landscape and dates quickly. assemblyai.com/blog/voice-agent-architecture
LiveKit — Voice agent architecture: STT, LLM and TTS pipelines explained
The clearest diagram-level explanation of the cascade. Pairs with their sequential-pipeline post, which is about ordering the stages. livekit.com/blog/voice-agent-architecture-stt-llm-tts-pipelines-explained
ElevenLabs — Voice agent latency optimization
The techniques rather than the architecture: streaming, sentence chunking, overlapping stages. Source for the sentence-buffer rules in Chapter 49 — punctuation plus whitespace, exclude abbreviations and decimals, minimum chunk length. elevenlabs.io/blog/voice-agent-latency-optimization
arXiv 2508.04721 — Toward Low-Latency End-to-End Voice Agents for Telecommunications
Streaming ASR, quantised LLMs and real-time TTS in a telecoms setting. The closest published work to this project's shape; useful if the whole pipeline is ever brought in-house. arxiv.org/html/2508.04721v1

Turn-taking, barge-in and endpointing  Part IX

● LiveKit — Turn detection for voice agents: VAD, endpointing, and model-based detection
The reference for Chapter 47. Compares the three methods honestly including their failure modes, and carries the 800 ms-silence arithmetic. Recommends STT endpointing as the production default. livekit.com/blog/turn-detection-voice-agents-vad-endpointing-model-based-detection
LiveKit — Turns overview (documentation)
The implementation view: the five turn-detection modes an agent session supports. Read this rather than the blog post if the layer is actually being built. docs.livekit.io/agents/build/turns/
LiveKit — Using a transformer to improve end of turn detection
The ~135M-parameter local turn-detection model, and why semantic detection combined with acoustic VAD produces natural pacing. blog.livekit.io/using-a-transformer-to-improve-end-of-turn-detection
Hamming — Voice Agent Interruption Handling: Barge-In, Backchannels, Turn Detection
Source for the per-vertical silence thresholds (250/400/600 ms) and for the separation between turn detection and interruption handling that Chapter 48 rests on. hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-interruption-handling-runbook
AssemblyAI — How intelligent turn detection solves the biggest challenge in voice agent development
The interim/final/end-of-speech distinction in Chapter 46, from the recogniser's side. assemblyai.com/blog/turn-detection-endpointing-voice-agent
Pipecat — Overview, and the repository
The frame-and-processor pipeline model. Worth reading for the architecture even with no intention of adopting it — typed frames flowing through processors that pass through what they do not understand is a good shape for this problem in any language. docs.pipecat.ai/pipecat/learn/overview  ·  github.com/pipecat-ai/pipecat

Task-oriented dialogue, slots and state  Parts I–II

W3C — Voice Extensible Markup Language (VoiceXML) 2.0
The origin. Forms, field item variables, mixed initiative, noinput/nomatch counters, <filled>. Skim the syntax; read the dialogue model. Twenty-five years old and still the clearest statement of the problem. w3.org/TR/voicexml20/
Definitive VoiceXML — Appendix: the Form Interpretation Algorithm
The FIA written out step by step. The algorithm any slot-filling implementation converges on; worth reading once before designing one. oreilly.com/library/view/definitive-voicexmltm/0130463450/0130463450_app03.html
Rasa — Forms, and Slot validation actions
The two-tier validation split in Chapter 8: domain-level constraints in the schema, business rules in validate_<slot>. Also the cleanest treatment of custom slot extraction. legacy-docs-oss.rasa.com/docs/rasa/forms/  ·  rasa.com/docs/rasa-pro/nlu-based-assistants/slot-validation-actions
Google — Dialogflow CX: Parameters
Pages as steps, parameters as slots, required versus optional, initial prompt and reprompt handlers. The modern restatement of the FIA, and the source for the ordering behaviour in Chapter 5's table. docs.cloud.google.com/dialogflow/cx/docs/concept/parameter
● arXiv 2501.11613 — Conversation Routines: A Prompt Engineering Framework for Task-Oriented Dialog Systems
Business logic encoded in prompts as structured natural language, with domain experts writing workflows and engineers writing the tools. The closest published framing of what Part III describes. Read the limitations section too — it is honest about unpredictable outputs and the difficulty of optimising specialised logic this way. arxiv.org/html/2501.11613v3
SpokenWOZ — A Large-Scale Speech-Text Benchmark for Spoken Task-Oriented Dialogue Agents
The source of the number in Chapter 6. Best joint goal accuracy 25.65%; best end-to-end request completion 52.1%. If one citation in this book is worth following, it is this one — it is the empirical argument against letting a model hold the state. openreview.net/forum?id=viktK3nO5b
MultiWOZ 2.4 — annotation corrections for state-tracking evaluation
The written-dialogue counterpart, for the contrast: ~57% joint goal accuracy on clean typed text against 25.65% on speech. Also a good illustration of how much benchmark numbers move when annotations are fixed. arxiv.org/pdf/2104.00773
arXiv 2505.14299 — Empowering LLMs in Task-Oriented Dialogues: A Domain-Independent Multi-Agent Framework
Decomposition into task-specific agents. Relevant if the single-call-per-turn design ever needs splitting; also the source for the documented hallucination problem on slots the conversation never mentioned. arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14299

Prompting, context and agent architecture  Parts I, III

● Anthropic — Effective context engineering for AI agents
Chapter 14 is mostly this. The “right altitude” framing, the smallest-set-of-high-signal-tokens principle, sectioned prompts, curated canonical few-shot examples rather than edge-case dumps, and compaction for long-running work. anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents
● Anthropic — Building effective agents
The workflow-versus-agent taxonomy in Chapter 4 and the five named workflow patterns. The restraint advice — start simple, add agentic machinery only when simpler approaches demonstrably fall short — is the part most worth taking seriously. anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents
Anthropic — Writing effective tools for AI agents
Applies to capability descriptions as much as to tools. Source for the test in Chapter 14: if a human engineer cannot say which one applies, neither can the model. anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents
● OpenAI — Realtime Prompting Guide
The densest practical source in this list, and the basis of Chapter 13. The eight prompt sections, bullets over paragraphs, capitalised rules, sample-phrase imitation, pinning the output language, variety rules, tool preambles — and the “inaudible”/“unintelligible” anecdote that justifies treating prompts as tested artefacts. developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/realtime_prompting_guide
OpenAI — Voice agents guide, and the realtime-agents metaprompt
The guide is the architectural overview; the metaprompt is a worked example of a voice-agent system prompt with a conversation-flow state machine written in plain language. The metaprompt is the more useful of the two. developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/voice-agents  ·  github.com/openai/openai-realtime-agents
● HumanLayer — 12-Factor Agents
Five of the twelve carry this book's spine: own your prompts (2), own your context window (3), unify execution and business state (5), own your control flow (8), and make the agent a stateless reducer (12). Short, opinionated, and the most quotable framing of “the model may choose the action; the application owns the loop”. github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents

Serving: vLLM, structured output, caching  Part IV

vLLM — Structured Outputs
The primary reference for Chapter 22. guided_json, guided_choice, guided_regex, guided_grammar, and the backend selection. Check the version: the parameter names and defaults have moved between releases. docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/features/structured_outputs.html
vLLM — Automatic Prefix Caching (design docs)
How the block hash table works, and why blocks are independent rather than a tree. Read alongside the entry below, which is more practical. docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/design/prefix_caching/
sankalp — How prompt caching works: paged attention, automatic prefix caching, and practical tips
Where the operationally important detail in Chapter 26 comes from: only complete 16-token blocks are cached, so a prefix ending mid-block gets no credit. Also the 60–80% time-to-first-token reduction figure for long shared system prompts. sankalp.bearblog.dev/how-prompt-caching-works/
Red Hat — Structured outputs in vLLM: guiding AI responses
A gentler introduction than the vLLM docs, with the production framing. Good for deciding whether to migrate; the official docs are better once the decision is made. developers.redhat.com/articles/2025/06/03/structured-outputs-vllm-guiding-ai-responses
SqueezeBits — Guided decoding performance on vLLM and SGLang; and Automatic prefix caching
Measured rather than described. The XGrammar caching behaviour and the context-dependent/context-independent token split that makes Chapter 22's “few, stable schemas” rule matter. blog.squeezebits.com/guided-decoding-performance-vllm-sglang
Fireworks — Turn your LLM into a calibrated classifier
Chapter 23's argument, with the work behind it. The bimodal-extremes and class-dependent-skew failure patterns, and what calibration actually requires. fireworks.ai/blog/Finetuning-LLMs-as-Classifiers
Logprobs in production: four things you can actually do with them
The practical alternative to self-reported confidence. Short, concrete, and the right starting point if confidence is ever going to gate a branch. dev.to/gabrielanhaia/logprobs-in-production-4-things-you-can-actually-do-with-them-3hg6

Reliability  Part V

Standard Webhooks (Svix, with Twilio among its partners)
The webhook-id / webhook-timestamp / webhook-signature triple, and the explicit recommendation to use webhook-id as the idempotency key. Cited here through secondary sources rather than read directly. standardwebhooks.com
Hookdeck — Guide to Twilio webhooks: features and best practices
Source for the 15-second response ceiling, the I-Twilio-Idempotency-Token header, and the queue-first pattern that ceiling forces. hookdeck.com/webhooks/platforms/twilio-webhooks-features-and-best-practices-guide
Exactly-once webhook processing: the pattern every developer gets wrong
At-least-once delivery plus idempotent effects, and the partial-failure problem when a handler has several side effects. The framing Chapter 28 uses. dev.to/eventdock/exactly-once-webhook-processing-the-pattern-every-developer-gets-wrong-35ca
Redis — Agent memory developer guide, and the memory layer guide for redis-rs
Hot/cold state separation, TTL management, the rolling turn window stored as one JSON value so the session loads in a single operation. The redis-rs page is the directly applicable one. redis.io/docs/latest/develop/ai/context-engine/agent-memory/developer-guide/  ·  redis.io/docs/latest/develop/use-cases/memory-layer/rust/

Voice interaction design  Part VI

Google — Voice agent design best practices (Dialogflow CX)
The most concrete of the voice-UX sources and the one worth reading end to end. Source for the three-strike rule in Chapter 35 and for the confirm-only-what-is-important principle in Chapter 33. docs.cloud.google.com/dialogflow/cx/docs/concept/voice-agent-design
Gradium — How to make TTS pronounce numbers, dates, currencies and phone numbers correctly
Chapter 34's table, essentially. Includes the 04/05/2026 ambiguity and the observation that these patterns were rare in training data and are exactly what a form produces. gradium.ai/content/how-to-make-tts-pronounce-numbers-dates-currencies-phone-numbers
Picovoice — SSML for production TTS; ElevenLabs — TTS best practices
The <say-as> mechanics, and the caveat that modern neural engines normalise most text without SSML — which is what makes the failure quiet rather than obvious. picovoice.ai/blog/ssml-text-to-speech/  ·  elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech/best-practices
Replicant — When to hand off to a human: setting effective AI escalation rules
The trigger list in Chapter 36, including the elapsed-time rule (5–10 minutes for voice) that fires regardless of other signals. replicant.com/blog/when-to-hand-off-to-a-human-how-to-set-effective-ai-escalation-rules
Haptik — Warm transfer and escalation design between AI and human agents
Cold / warm / conferenced, and the finding that matters most: pass a structured handoff card, not a free-form summary, and never make the caller re-authenticate. haptik.ai/blog/warm-transfer-escalation-design-between-ai-human-agents

Security and compliance  Parts III, VIII

OWASP — LLM Prompt Injection Prevention Cheat Sheet
The defensive measures in Chapter 17. Read it for what it says is not a control as much as for what is. cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html
OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications
Prompt injection ranked first; excessive agency and sensitive information disclosure the other two that dominate real 2026 agent incident logs. The framing for “what would it cost if the caller could choose this capability freely?” promptfoo.dev/docs/red-team/owasp-llm-top-10/
Hamming — PII redaction for voice agent transcripts: compliance and architecture
Chapter 43's central claim — redact before storage, compliance is architectural — and the NER-on-the-stream mechanism. Also the point that redacting the transcript while keeping the audio is not redaction. hamming.ai/resources/pii-redaction-voice-agents-compliance-architecture-guide
Softcery — EU voice AI regulations: a founder's guide (AI Act, GDPR, call recording)
The three regimes in one place, including recording consent and the 30–90 day QA retention convention. A survey rather than an authority; treat it as a map of what to ask a lawyer about. softcery.com/lab/eu-voice-ai-regulations-founders-guide
Resemble — EU AI Act Article 50 compliance checklist; Famulor — What your AI phone agent must say
Chapter 45. The 2 August 2026 effective date, and the requirement that voice disclosure be audible, in plain language, at the latest at the first interaction, naming that it is a machine and on whose behalf it is calling. resemble.ai/resources/eu-ai-act-article-50-compliance-checklist-for-providers-and-deployers  ·  famulor.io/blog/eu-ai-act-article-50-what-your-ai-phone-agent-must-say

Prompt lifecycle  Chapter 18

LaunchDarkly — Prompt versioning and management; Agenta — Prompt versioning: the complete guide
Two statements of the same consensus: prompts in git, one per file, reviewed like code, pinned to exact versions in production, templated rather than string-concatenated, and decoupled from the deployment cycle. This project already does the hard half; these are useful for the parts it does not yet do. launchdarkly.com/blog/prompt-versioning-and-management/  ·  agenta.ai/blog/prompt-versioning-guide

In-repository sources

● H:\rust\ai_autocalls_rust
The most useful reference in this list, because it is a working implementation of Part II by the same author. In reading order: app/src/events/mod.rs (the whole call flow as one enum, with the vocabulary and the naming rule), docs/EXTENDING.md (what can be added without writing code), app/src/call/session.rs (what a call remembers and why Redis), app/src/forms/flow.rs (the stage machine), app/src/forms/value.rs (typed answers and per-kind read-back), app/src/forms/delivery.rs (commit before you say it is done), app/src/forms/parsers/ (one file per answer kind).
● H:\rust\ai_assistant — context.md and the source
Every measured number attributed to “this machine” comes from context.md: the 0.6–2.8 s turn times, the 30-second timeout incident, the hot-reload byte counts, the RUST_LOG trap. app/src/vllm.rs is the best-documented file in either repository and the style model for this book.
