Voice realities

Everything in this Part follows from one fact: the caller cannot see, cannot scroll, and cannot re-read. A spoken sentence exists for two seconds and then only in someone's memory. That constraint invalidates most of what is known about designing forms, and the discipline that replaces it is older and better documented than the model at the centre of the system.

32 The caller cannot scroll

A handful of rules, each of which is a consequence of the same constraint.

One question per turn
Two questions in one sentence gets one answer, and it is ambiguous which question it answered. This is not a model limitation; it is how people respond to compound questions.
At most three options, and put them last
Recall of a spoken list collapses past three, and the last item is remembered best. “Would you like morning, afternoon, or evening?”, never a list of six times.
The question goes at the end
Context first, question last. A caller who hears the question first stops listening to the context and answers immediately.
Acknowledge, then proceed
A one-word acknowledgement before the next question tells the caller they were heard. Without it every turn feels like the system might not have registered the last one.
Never read out what belongs on a screen
Reference numbers, URLs, opening hours for seven days. If it has to be conveyed, it goes to an SMS or an email, and the call says so.

Say what is happening

The caller must always know whether the system is listening, working, or speaking. Silence is the ambiguous state and it is the one that breaks conversations — a caller who is not sure whether they were heard will speak again, and the barge-in that follows costs a whole turn. → §IX, Chapter 48

Where an action takes a noticeable time — a lookup, an availability check — the sentence that precedes it is doing real work: “Let me check that for you.” It converts dead air into an expected pause. The realtime prompting guidance calls these tool preambles and treats them as part of the tool's definition rather than as decoration, which is the right place for them.

33 Confirmation: what, and how much

Confirmations prevent mistakes and cost turns. The working principle is narrow: confirm what is expensive to get wrong, and nothing else. High-risk actions — payments, cancellations, account changes — are confirmed clearly; low-risk lookups are not confirmed at all.

The three strategies

StrategySounds likeCostsUse for
None “And how many people?” 0 turns Low-stakes, easily corrected later, high recognition confidence
Implicit “The second of October — and how many people?” 0 turns The default. Most collected slots
Explicit “That's the second of October. Is that right?” 1 turn Irreversible actions, identifiers, money, anything read from a lookup

Implicit confirmation is the technique worth internalising: the value is read back inside the next question, so the caller can object but is not required to agree. It costs nothing and catches most recognition errors, because a caller who hears the wrong date will interrupt.

The failure mode of over-confirming is not merely slowness. A caller asked “is that right?” five times stops listening and says yes reflexively, which means the sixth confirmation — the one that mattered — is worthless. Confirmation is a budget, and spending it on a party size means not having it for a payment.

Batch the explicit confirmation

Rather than confirming each field as it is collected, collect them and read the whole thing back once at the end. One turn instead of four, and the caller hears the booking as a booking rather than as a sequence of disconnected facts.

The cost is the trap from Chapter 9: the review stage must accept a correction to one field, not just a yes or a no. “No, the date's wrong” is a yes to everything else.

In this repo

ai_autocalls_rust models both: ConfirmingField { index } for per-field explicit confirmation and ReviewingSummary for the batch at the end, with form_review.rs handling the latter. Per-field confirmation is therefore a property of the field, which is where it belongs — in the manifest, next to the type and the validator (Chapter 16).

One detail from that codebase is worth copying exactly: yes/no goes through the ordinary parser, and that parser checks negatives first. “That's not right” and German “stimmt nicht” both contain a positive word. The same function confirms a date of birth and accepts a final booking, so getting the order wrong there is not a detail.

34 Reading values back

Text-to-speech engines handle ordinary prose well and handle exactly the patterns a form produces badly: identifiers, dates in regional formats, phone numbers with international prefixes, currency. These were rare in training data and are the entire output of a booking flow.

The architectural rule

The model emits structure. Code renders speech.

A model asked to produce a spoken form of a date will produce a good one most of the time. “Most of the time” is unacceptable for the one sentence whose whole purpose is letting the caller verify a value. Rendering a typed value into speech is a total function over a small set of types, it is testable, and it cannot drift.

This is the general form of the argument in forms/value.rs: read-back is a method on the value, per kind, and the compiler lists every match arm so a new answer kind cannot forget it.

The table

The date trap, specifically

04/05/2026 is the 4th of May in most of the world and the 5th of April in the United States. A system serving de|en|ru has three conventions in play. The resolution is that a date never exists as a formatted string inside the system — it is a date type from the moment it is parsed until the moment it is rendered, and it is rendered differently for storage, for display and for speech.

Confirm digit by digit, then check the whole

When reading an identifier back for verification, group it: “that's I, 5, D, F, 3 — I5DF3”. The spelled version is what the caller checks against the letters in front of them; the whole version is what they recognise. Either alone is worse than both.

Trap

Modern neural synthesisers normalise most text acceptably without SSML, which is why this is easy to skip in testing with clean English input. It degrades quietly: a German date read with English conventions, a reference code pronounced as a word, a price read as a decimal number. None of these raise an error and all of them produce a caller who confirms the wrong value — which is worse than a caller who could not hear it, because the system now has a confirmed wrong value.

35 No-match, no-input, and the third strike

Two failures, and they need different sentences.

No-input
Silence. They did not hear, are distracted, or the audio path failed. The right response repeats the question, usually verbatim.
No-match
They spoke and it could not be understood. The right response changes — repeating the same question is the single most frustrating behaviour a phone system has.

The escalation

AttemptNo-inputNo-match
1Repeat verbatimRephrase, shorter
2Rephrase, add the formatNarrow it: offer two or three choices, or ask for a digit
3Hand off. Do not ask a fourth time

Three is the documented convention — Dialogflow CX's voice-agent guidance is explicit about a maximum of three no-match/no-input events per page, escalating to a human on the third. It is not arbitrary: by the third failure the problem is usually the audio path, the accent, or a question the caller cannot answer, and none of those improve with a fourth attempt.

Narrowing beats repeating

Attempt 2 for a no-match should reduce the answer space, not restate the question. An open question that failed twice will fail a third time; a closed one might not.

ask:      "What date would you like?"
no-match: "Sorry - what day were you thinking of?"
no-match: "Let's try this - would you prefer this week or next?"
                                              // two options, not a date

This also changes the parser in play: a choice parser is far more forgiving than a date parser, so the narrowed question is more likely to succeed for reasons beyond the wording.

In this repo

FormProgress::attempts is this counter, and its doc comment carries the rule that is easiest to get wrong: it is not incremented when the caller asks a reasonable question instead of answering — “they did not fail, they asked something”. Without that exception, a caller who asks two clarifying questions about a form is transferred to a human for being engaged. The counter resets whenever an answer lands.

Rule

The re-prompt must differ from the prompt, and the third failure ends the attempt. Put the variety in the prompt layer as an explicit instruction plus two differently-shaped examples (Chapter 13); put the counting and the escalation in code (Chapter 8). Neither works alone.

36 Handing off to a human

The three shapes

Cold transfer
Route and disconnect. Fine for department routing where context does not matter.
Warm transfer with introduction
The agent briefs the human before connecting. The default for anything where what has already been said shapes what the human says next.
Conferenced
The system stays on the line and keeps working — notes, CRM updates, follow-up scheduling — while the human talks.

What triggers one

  • Confidence below the per-intent threshold (Chapter 23).
  • Three failures on one step (Chapter 35).
  • Sentiment — and here anger_level earns its place.
  • Policy: refunds over a limit, legal language, a vulnerable caller.
  • Elapsed time. A voice interaction running past 5–10 minutes without resolution escalates regardless of every other signal.
  • The caller asked. This one is absolute and needs no threshold.

Hand over a structure, not a paragraph

The single most actionable finding in the hand-off literature: pass structured context, not free-form notes. The human wants a card with the fields that matter — account identifier, request type, what has been tried, sentiment — because a free-form summary wastes the first thirty seconds of their attention.

call_id       9f2c-...             turns          14
caller        Maria Kovac          sentiment      frustrated (0.7)
verified      customer_id C-88213  (lookup, turn 4)
intent        reschedule_appointment
collected     date 2026-10-02 (confirmed), party_size - (3 failures)
tried         narrowed to this week / next week; both no-match
reason        attempt limit on party_size

Note the shape: it is the slot map plus provenance plus the attempt counters. Every field on that card already exists in the state designed in Part II. The hand-off card is not a feature to build later; it is a rendering of state the system already holds, which is one of the better arguments for holding it properly.

Rule

Never make the caller re-authenticate after a transfer. Re-verification after hand-off is the most common complaint about escalation, and the fix is to pass the authentication state forward as part of the card. A Verified slot (Chapter 7) is precisely the thing that can be passed with confidence — which is why the distinction was worth a type.

37 Language, and staying in it

The schema asks for language_detected with values de|en|ru, which means language is currently decided per turn. For a stateless classifier that is the only option. For a call it is the wrong default.

Sticky, with an explicit switch

The language of a call is a property of the call. Detect it on the first substantive turn, record it on the session, and use it for everything afterwards — including everything already asked. A caller who says a proper noun that reads as German should not get a German confirmation of an English booking.

Per-turn detection has a specific failure mode: short utterances. “Ja”, “OK”, “Maria Kovac” carry almost no language signal, and a per-turn detector will flip on them — usually at the confirmation step, which is the worst possible moment.

In this repo

CallSession::language in ai_autocalls_rust carries exactly this decision, with the reasoning in the field's comment: recorded on the session as well as on each request “so that a caller who switches their language selector mid-call does not end up with a form half in each — the session's language wins for anything already asked.” Keep language_detected in the schema as a signal; do not let it be the decision.

What has to be translated, and what does not

LayerMultilingual?
